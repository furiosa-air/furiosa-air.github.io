Elicitation or Expansion?

Demystifying Gains from Agent Harness Optimization

Younghyun Kim1*, Wooseok Seo2*, Minseo Kim3, Seunghyuk Oh1, Minjae Lee1, Donghoon Kim1, Hyung Il Koo1, Jaehyung Kim2†, Wonjun Kang1†

1FuriosaAI 2Yonsei University 3University of California, Berkeley

*Equal contribution †Co-corresponding authors

arXiv Code Dataset
What we did
We bring pass@k analysis to agent harness optimization,
comparing optimized harnesses against larger models and higher reasoning effort across attempt budgets and task difficulty.
What we found
A lead at pass@1 can reverse with more attempts.
Even when optimized harnesses approach or exceed stronger baselines overall, they do not surpass them on the hard subsets we evaluate.

01 · Background

Same weights.
Wider reach?

Harness optimization rewrites the code around a fixed model, including its prompts, context, tool use, and execution logic, and reports large gains. A higher success rate can mean two different things.

The difference matters for methods that learn from their own successes, such as self-training and recursive self-improvement. For them, even rare successes on previously unreachable tasks can seed further search or learning.

02 · The lens

A lens from RL.
Now on harness optimization.

RL studies compare models before and after training with pass@k: pass@1 gains can shrink or reverse at larger sampling budgets (Yue et al., 2025). We bring the same analysis to harnesses, with three comparisons.

Step 1

Across attempt budgets

pass@1 measures reliability; pass@k, the expected share of tasks solved at least once in k attempts, measures coverage.

Step 2

Against stronger interventions

The baseline harness with a larger model or higher reasoning effort.

Step 3

By task difficulty

Using the benchmarks' own difficulty labels for HotpotQA and AppWorld.

  • Text classification GPT-OSS-20B vs. 120B
  • HotpotQA Qwen3-8B vs. 32B
  • AppWorld GPT-OSS-120B, medium vs. high effort
The estimator, precisely

pass@k estimator

\[ \widehat{\mathrm{pass@}k} \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\,1-\binom{n-c_i}{k}\Big/\binom{n}{k}\right], \qquad 1 \le k \le n \]

Each of the \(N\) tasks is attempted \(n\) times, and \(c_i\) counts the successes on task \(x_i\). An attempt is one complete execution, including every model call and tool interaction; \(n\) is 64 for text classification and 128 for HotpotQA and AppWorld. Success is scored by binary correctness for text classification, answer containment confirmed by an LLM judge for HotpotQA, and arity-fixed task goal completion for AppWorld. Harnesses are optimized with Meta-Harness.

03 · Across budgets

A first-try lead
need not be a coverage lead.

On LawBench, the optimized 20B harness leads the 120B model by 16.05 pp at pass@1, but falls behind from \(k = 11\). On all three text benchmarks, it ends below the 120B model at pass@64.

Text classification: mostly elicitation.

Optimization mostly makes existing successes more frequent. On LawBench, it keeps 49 of the 53 tasks the baseline solves within 64 attempts, loses 4, and newly solves 7.

Even among candidates from one run.

The same split appears among the harness candidates that a single optimization run generates on HotpotQA. The candidate with the highest pass@1 is not the one with the highest pass@128, and two candidates can trade places against Qwen3-32B as the budget grows.

04 · Key finding · Task difficulty

Close in aggregate.
Never ahead on hard tasks.

On HotpotQA and AppWorld, the selected optimized harnesses approach or exceed a larger model or higher reasoning effort across all tasks. On the hard subsets, none exceeds it at any evaluated budget up to 128 attempts.

Harness gains trail scaling on hard tasks

On hard tasks, harness optimization yields smaller mean pass@1 gains than scaling on all three benchmarks (dataset-level difficulty for text).

Every candidate falls short

All 40 candidates from one HotpotQA optimization run stay below the 32B model on hard questions.

Hard-focused re-optimization did not close the gap

Re-optimizing the HotpotQA harness on a hard-focused task mixture raises hard-task pass@1 (56.88% → 59.28%; 32B: 59.51%) but leaves pass@128 unchanged at 73.17% (32B: 78.05%).

05 · Looking ahead

Measure reliability
and reach separately.

Check a harness gain at the attempt budget you will use, against a larger model or more reasoning effort, and on hard tasks. Achieving consistent capability expansion on hard tasks remains an open challenge for harness optimization, especially when successful executions support recursive self-improvement or evolutionary search. Our results are bounded by the evaluated benchmarks, solver families, and optimization runs.
